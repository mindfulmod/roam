### Holy cities notes

- **Mecca** (Islam): Only Muslims may enter; the Hajj and Umrah need a pilgrim visa.
- **Medina** (Islam): The Prophet's Mosque and the area around it are for Muslims only; the rest of the city is open.
- **Jerusalem** (Judaism, Christianity, Islam): Western Wall, Church of the Holy Sepulchre, Al-Aqsa compound (non-Muslim visiting hours are limited).
- **Bethlehem** (Christianity): Church of the Nativity.
- **Vatican City** (Christianity): Inside the Rome guide.
- **Karbala** (Islam (Shia)): Shrine of Imam Husayn; Arbaeen draws millions.
- **Najaf** (Islam (Shia)): Shrine of Imam Ali.
- **Mashhad** (Islam (Shia)): Shrine of Imam Reza; inner courtyards are for Muslims only.
- **Konya** (Islam (Sufi)): Tomb of Rumi.
- **Varanasi** (Hinduism): Ghats on the Ganges; Sarnath, where the Buddha first taught, is nearby.
- **Amritsar** (Sikhism): The Golden Temple; head covering required, open to all.
- **Rishikesh** (Hinduism): Ganga Aarti and ashrams.
- **Bodh Gaya** (Buddhism): Where the Buddha reached enlightenment.
- **Lumbini** (Buddhism): Birthplace of the Buddha.
- **Kandy** (Buddhism): Temple of the Sacred Tooth Relic.
- **Lhasa** (Buddhism): Jokhang and Potala Palace; Tibet permits required.
- **Lourdes** (Christianity): Marian sanctuary and grotto.
- **Fátima** (Christianity): Marian sanctuary.
- **Santiago de Compostela** (Christianity): End of the Camino de Santiago.
- **Assisi** (Christianity): Basilica of St Francis.
- **Koyasan** (Buddhism): Temple lodgings and Okunoin cemetery.
- **Ise** (Shinto): Ise Grand Shrine.
- **Haifa** (Bahá'í): Bahá'í Gardens and Shrine of the Báb.
- **Lalibela** (Christianity): Rock-hewn churches.
